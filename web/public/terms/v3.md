## 7. Everything is public and permanent

*In short: anyone can read what you write here, forever. Don't post anything private.*

If a part is removed or taken down, it disappears from SwarmTales, from the contract's storage, and from future editions of the book. Copies in the blockchain's transaction history can't be erased by anyone, including us. Because of this, we can't delete or correct blockchain records on request, even where privacy laws would otherwise give you that right. Never post personal information about yourself or anyone else.

*In short: rights holders can ask us to take copied text down; writers can answer; repeat infringers are blocked.*

We respect copyright and respond to notices under the U.S. Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA).

**Sending a notice.** If you believe text on SwarmTales infringes your copyright, send a written notice to our designated agent ({{DMCA_AGENT}}, {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}) with:

1. your physical or electronic signature;
2. the work you say is infringed;

4. your name, address, telephone number and email;
5. a statement that you believe in good faith the use is not authorised by you, your agent, or the law;
6. a statement, under penalty of perjury, that the notice is accurate and that you own the right or may act for its owner.

**What happens.** We may hide the part at once and take it down for good. We record every hide and takedown publicly, with its reason, and may tell the writer. The "Report copied text" page also lets anyone flag copied text quickly.

**Counter-notice.** If your part was taken down and you believe it was a mistake or you had the right to post it, you may send our agent a counter-notice with: your signature; the part that was removed and where it was; a statement under penalty of perjury that you believe in good faith it was removed by mistake or misidentification; your name, address and telephone number; and your consent to the jurisdiction of the federal court for your address (or, outside the United States, any judicial district where we may be found) and to accept service from the person who sent the notice. **False claims.** Knowingly misrepresenting that something infringes, or that it was removed by mistake, can make you liable for damages (17 U.S.C. § 512(f)).

**Repeat infringers.** Each takedown is a strike against the wallet credited for the text. When a wallet reaches the strike limit set in the Stories contract (five at launch), it can no longer open stories, write, suggest, or report on SwarmTales. We may also block a wallet sooner where infringement is clear and deliberate.

**Automatic checks.** SwarmTales checks new writing automatically and may hide a part while it is looked at. A writer may answer by naming the source. Automatic checks can be wrong in both directions; they don't decide whether something is legal.
